Project A · SCE WG1

The semantics of economic models.

Each toolkit team states formally what its model files mean — an AI-readable, deterministic statement, independent of code and solver, of which economic objects exist, what stands for what, what is written down, and when the interpretation holds.

Chaired by Akshay Shanker and Matt McKay

Working toward a journal special issue · read the full proposal

01 · Why

A model's meaning is implicit

When economists compute a model, its precise meaning is distributed across prose, notation, calibration, and code. The paper says we solve the following model, but its equations underdetermine both what the code computes and what the computation means, leaving the reader to fill the gaps. The same gaps confront whoever rebuilds or extends it, so results are hard to cross-verify and implementations hard to interoperate.

Convention breaks down once AI writes the code

Professional convention has helped trained modelers interpret and replicate one another's work. But economists increasingly use language models to write, modify, and translate modeling code, and that output is probabilistic by construction. Unverified, it can compute a different model from the one intended. Verifying it requires a statement, independent of the code, of which objects are computed and which relations among them are enforced — a semantic ontology.

02 · The proposal

Proposed special issue

A special issue collecting semantic ontologies for the domains of computational economics: modeling languages, toolkits, empirical methods, and model classes. Each paper takes one domain of study, states its semantic ontology, and demonstrates it on worked examples of the team's own choosing. The working group will submit a subset of the papers, an open call will invite the rest, and a comparison paper, written jointly by the participating teams, will close the issue.

With semantic ontologies in place, translating models between toolkits — and generating new modelling research — become operations a language model can carry out and be checked on cheaply, rather than manual recoding.

03 · Domains of study

Each paper addresses one domain of study — a modeling language, a toolkit, an empirical method, a model class, or a combination of these.

Modeling languages

A fixed grammar in which a complete model is written as a file, as in Dynare's model language or Dolo's YAML model files.

Toolkits

A collection of construction calls and classes from which a model is assembled in a programming language, as in HARK's agent classes, the sequence-space Jacobian toolkit, or the VFI Toolkit.

Estimation and empirical methods

A method that takes a model to data — which measured objects stand for the model's quantities, which classifications organize them, and which transformations construct the inputs to estimation or calibration.

Model classes

A family of models built from the same kinds of objects, as in heterogeneous-agent macroeconomies or overlapping-generations economies.

04 · Research themes

Every paper answers two core questions: it constructs a semantic ontology for its domain, and it establishes that ontology's metatheory — statements about the ontology rather than components of it.

The three components

  • Ontology. What is assumed to exist.
  • Denotation. What stands for, and relates to, what.
  • Syntax. What is written down.

Domains differ in how important each component is, and some — a model class, for instance — may not have a syntax at all.

The metatheory

The metatheory states when the interpretation holds (well-posedness) and what preserves it (equivalence, adequacy, and convergence).

05 · Organization

The working group's members include developers of Dynare, HARK, and the VFI Toolkit, and its papers come from its subgroups. Papers pass an internal review within the working group and then the journal's ordinary external refereeing. We expect six to eight papers, with a session at the Society's conference between submission and revision.

Guest editors — a lead editor and at least one co-editor, neither submitting to the issue — are drawn from outside the working group. Every paper, including the comparison paper, is handled by the guest editors, subject to the journal's final editorial authority.
